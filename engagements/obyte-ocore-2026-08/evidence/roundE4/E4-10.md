# E4-10 — Field-notes lessons package (cross-engagement, abstract, portable)

Drafted for `docs/field-notes.md` (newest-first; same "what we hit → what we changed → the portable
rule" format). These are the lessons of the obyte-ocore-2026-08 escalation phase — the phase whose
primary outcome was **refuting the engagement's own promoted finding**, which is itself the lesson.

---

## 2026-08-11 — Five lessons from the escalation phase of a DAG-ledger audit

### 1. A "real-path" drive is only as real as the state it seeds (the synthesized-state lesson, applied to our own finding)

**What we hit.** A tps-fee poisoning finding was "confirmed through the real path" by a drive that
seeded a synthetic stable-unit cache shaped like a 30-unit flood window. The drive measured a
settlement fee of ~10.7% of the total supply and an honest author's balance driven to −10.7% — and
this became a promoted finding (Low) after passing all six lifecycle gates. Then the escalation
phase built the real two-node network and ran a genuine 20-unit burst: actual fees were 0–1 base
units and the author's balance went POSITIVE (+10/unit, the estimation-multiplier overpayment
credited back). Re-running the drive's scenario through the real storage path with the real
stabilization sequence (one MCI at a time) produced fee=17 at every step. The count>=30 state the
drive seeded — last ball at mci 0 while the tip sat at mci 30 — is **unreachable**: the settlement
runs after the unit's own MCI is balled, so every settling unit's window contains only itself
(count=1).

**What we changed.** Demoted the finding; the honest-author-freeze impact claim is refuted. The
residual truth is self-frosting economics (an attacker burns ~10.7% of the supply on its own chain
for no collateral damage), a conditional MySQL-only watermark double-application, and a fee-curve
design note. The demotion record names the same error class as the earlier crash-family refutation:
**a crash (or a poisoning) on a seeded state is not a reachable crash (or poisoning).** We had
applied this to a hunter's PoC; the escalation phase forced us to apply it to our own gate-passing
finding.

**The portable rule.** A "real-path" call on a seeded cache proves the *arithmetic* of a window,
not the *reachability* of the state it models — it is one step better than a unit test and one step
worse than the network. Before believing any window/count/aggregation claim, (a) draw the state
mutations that define the window, (b) verify where the settlement runs relative to them, and
(c) if a real network or the documented state machine cannot produce the seeded state, treat the
impact as a model error, not an attack. Impact claims that have never survived a real-state-machine
run are provisional regardless of how many gates the package passed — gates validate internal
consistency, not reachability.

### 2. Escalation is bidirectional: the round built to push findings UP refuted one

**What we hit.** The escalation/chainability round — whose mandate was to drive the confirmed
findings to their maximum honest impact — instead refuted the engagement's own promoted finding.
The refutation was enabled by the exact infrastructure the original finding's verification lacked:
the two-node network with real stabilization. The original "confirmation" ran against a seeded
cache because the network didn't exist yet; when it existed, the claim died.

**What we changed.** Demoted the finding; corrected the coverage statement; recorded that the
adversarial round correcting the engagement's own record is the expected outcome, not a failure.

**The portable rule.** Budget the escalation round as a two-way check: the same evidence that
proves a ceiling also tests the foundation. If an impact claim was confirmed on a state machine
that was not yet built, the escalation round must re-run it on the real machine before any
severity is taken to a vendor. The round that "found nothing to escalate" but killed a bad claim
is a successful round.

### 3. SQL-engine semantic claims must be executed, not assumed

**What we hit.** A "STRONG" consensus-divergence claim: `+sequence='good'` on Oracle MySQL coerces
the TEXT column to a number, making the predicate match all rows, changing double-spend
resolution, and diverging ball hashes between DB backends. The claim rested on documented coercion
semantics and was never executed. The refuter executed the exact expression on sqlite, MariaDB,
Oracle MySQL 8.0, and MySQL 5.7: unary plus coerces **only pure-integer strings** (`+'good'` stays
`'good'`), so the predicate is string-vs-string on every engine and correct everywhere. The claim
was refuted; the residual is a five-site hygiene note.

**What we changed.** The refutation; the rule that external-system semantic claims are hypotheses
until executed.

**The portable rule.** A semantic claim about a database engine (or any external system) is
documentation until it runs. Execute the exact expression on the exact engine. Where the engine is
unavailable, say so explicitly and never rate the claim STRONG — a documented-coercion assumption
that killed a "Critical-shaped" candidate cost the f2 hunter its strongest lead.

### 4. Directed families anchor to their briefs; the blind round finds what they missed

**What we hit.** Sixteen directed families covered validation, main-chain, signature, definition,
formula, assets, private payments, network protocol, storage/catchup, the AA engine, encoding,
races, fail-open, composer/signing, and economics — none covered the E2E device-message
decryption layer. A blind prior-free sweep (read the tree by mechanism, no briefs) found the one
new family of the whole engagement: a remote, zero-cost wallet crash via a scheduled uncaught
throw on an unknown device key (delivered through the hub mailbox; crash-loop confirmed 3/3).
It was quarantined as an adjacent surface, but it was the only mechanism the directed coverage
missed.

**What we changed.** The quarantine; the requirement that a blind no-new-family round precedes any
covered-dry verdict — with this engagement as the evidence for why it is a requirement, not a
bonus.

**The portable rule.** Directed coverage is anchoring in aggregate: sixteen hunters each doing
their brief perfectly still share the blind spot the briefs share. Run a mechanism-based,
prior-free sweep after the directed rounds and before any terminal coverage verdict. An honest
negative from the blind round is a deliverable; a new family from it is the round paying for
itself.

### 5. The settlement-ordering verification pattern: draw the ordering before believing the count

**What we hit.** The fee-poisoning claim assumed a window containing 30 units (the tip 30 MCIs
ahead of the last ball). The refutation came from a single source-level fact: in
`stabilizeMci`, the sequence is `markMcIndexStable` (resolve temp-bad) → `addBalls` (create the
ball for the MCI) → `updateTpsFees` (settle the unit). Because the unit's own MCI is balled
before its settlement runs, the settlement window `(last_ball_mci, this_mci]` contains only the
unit itself — count=1, always, under the 1-MCI-at-a-time stabilization. The seeded "lag state"
(ball at mci 0, tip at mci 30) is a state the ordering never produces.

**What we changed.** The demotion; the pattern as a standing verification step for
settlement/accounting/window claims.

**The portable rule.** For any settlement, accounting, or window claim in a ledger, first draw
the state-mutation ordering — what creates the window boundary, what runs the settlement, in what
order — then enumerate the states that ordering can produce. If the claimed attack needs a state
the ordering cannot produce, the claim is a model error, not an attack. The ordering is the
load-bearing fact; the arithmetic is downstream of it.

## Synthesis (the meta-lesson)

The five lessons are one lesson: **rigor is a property of the process, and the process must be
able to refute its own outputs.** The lifecycle gates kept the finding internally consistent; the
escalation round — with the real network, the executed SQL, the blind sweep, and the ordering
trace — kept it *true*. The prime directive ("default to disbelief") is not an aspiration about
other people's findings; the engagement's own promoted finding was the disbelief target, and the
process worked. The single highest-value engineering decision of the whole engagement was building
the two-node network before believing any settlement claim — infrastructure is an epistemic tool,
not a deliverable.
